Project Python Foundations: FoodHub Data Analysis¶

Context¶

The number of restaurants in New York is increasing day by day. Lots of students and busy professionals rely on those restaurants due to their hectic lifestyles. Online food delivery service is a great option for them. It provides them with good food from their favorite restaurants. A food aggregator company FoodHub offers access to multiple restaurants through a single smartphone app.

The app allows the restaurants to receive a direct online order from a customer. The app assigns a delivery person from the company to pick up the order after it is confirmed by the restaurant. The delivery person then uses the map to reach the restaurant and waits for the food package. Once the food package is handed over to the delivery person, he/she confirms the pick-up in the app and travels to the customer's location to deliver the food. The delivery person confirms the drop-off in the app after delivering the food package to the customer. The customer can rate the order in the app. The food aggregator earns money by collecting a fixed margin of the delivery order from the restaurants.

Objective¶

The food aggregator company has stored the data of the different orders made by the registered customers in their online portal. They want to analyze the data to get a fair idea about the demand of different restaurants which will help them in enhancing their customer experience. Suppose you are hired as a Data Scientist in this company and the Data Science team has shared some of the key questions that need to be answered. Perform the data analysis to find answers to these questions that will help the company to improve the business.

Data Description¶

The data contains the different data related to a food order. The detailed data dictionary is given below.

Data Dictionary¶

  • order_id: Unique ID of the order
  • customer_id: ID of the customer who ordered the food
  • restaurant_name: Name of the restaurant
  • cuisine_type: Cuisine ordered by the customer
  • cost_of_the_order: Cost of the order
  • day_of_the_week: Indicates whether the order is placed on a weekday or weekend (The weekday is from Monday to Friday and the weekend is Saturday and Sunday)
  • rating: Rating given by the customer out of 5
  • food_preparation_time: Time (in minutes) taken by the restaurant to prepare the food. This is calculated by taking the difference between the timestamps of the restaurant's order confirmation and the delivery person's pick-up confirmation.
  • delivery_time: Time (in minutes) taken by the delivery person to deliver the food package. This is calculated by taking the difference between the timestamps of the delivery person's pick-up confirmation and drop-off information

Conclusion and Recommendations¶

Question 17: What are your conclusions from the analysis? What recommendations would you like to share to help improve the business? (You can use cuisine type and feedback ratings to drive your business recommendations.) [6 marks]¶

Conclusions:¶

Data Overview:

  • Dataset contains 1,898 orders across diverse restaurants and cuisines with 9 key variables
  • 38.8% of orders lack ratings ('Not given'), limiting quality feedback; only 1,162 orders are rated
  • Strong weekend preference: 1,300 weekend orders vs. 598 weekday orders (69% vs. 31% split)

Service Performance:

  • Food preparation time averages 27.4 minutes (range: 20-35 min), showing consistent kitchen operations
  • Delivery time averages 24.2 minutes overall, with weekends ~5.9 minutes faster (22.5 min) than weekdays (28.3 min)
  • 89.5% of orders are delivered within 60 minutes total time (prep + delivery), a strong performance metric
  • Only 10.5% exceed 60 minutes, indicating generally efficient operations

Market Insights:

  • American cuisine dominates (600+ orders), followed by Japanese (500+) and Italian (300+)
  • Shake Shack is the market leader (219 orders, 11.5% share), with top 5 restaurants accounting for 37% of orders
  • 29.2% of orders exceed $20, with bimodal cost distribution suggesting two customer segments
  • Numeric variables show almost zero correlation: cost, time, and ratings are independently driven
  • High ratings (5.0) are predominant (61% of rated orders), suggesting customer satisfaction

Financial Performance:

  • Net revenue: $6,166.30 across all orders (~$3.25 average commission per order)
  • Commission structure: 25% for orders >$20, 15% for $5-$20, 0% for <$5
  • Premium orders (>$20) generate disproportionately high revenue despite being only 29% of orders

Customer Loyalty:

  • Top 3 customers placed only 32 orders combined (1.7% of total), indicating low concentration
  • Average customer places ~1.35 orders, showing significant opportunity for repeat order optimization

Recommendations:¶

1. Customer Retention & Loyalty Programs:

  • Implement targeted loyalty programs for top customers with exclusive deals and early access to new restaurants
  • Create a referral program to increase repeat orders (current average 1.35 orders per customer)
  • Offer discounts on weekday orders to balance the 69-31 weekend-weekday split
  • Target the 38.8% of non-rating customers with incentives to provide feedback (e.g., "Rate and Get Reward")

2. Restaurant Partner Strategy:

  • Provide promotional vouchers to high-performing restaurants (>50 ratings, avg rating >4.0)
  • Create tiered partnership model: reward consistent quality with lower commission rates
  • Support top restaurants with featured placements and priority logistics
  • Investigate American cuisine restaurants (weekend favorite) for special weekend promotions

3. Operational Excellence:

  • Weekday Delivery Optimization: Weekday deliveries take 26% longer. Investigate causes and increase delivery fleet during peak hours
  • Set aggressive SLA: Target <55 minutes total time (currently 89.5% hit 60-min target)
  • Focus on the 10.5% of outliers (>60 min total): categorize by cause and develop targeted solutions

4. Revenue Optimization:

  • Premium Segment Focus: Premium orders (>$20) generate 25% commission; focus marketing on upscale restaurants
  • Low-Cost Orders Problem: Orders <$5 generate 0% commission; establish minimum order value or delivery fee
  • Investigate the bimodal cost distribution: create targeted bundles for the $5-10 segment to drive order value

5. Data Quality & Analytics:

  • Address Rating Gap: 38.8% unrated orders limit quality assessment; implement automated rating requests and incentives
  • Correlations are near-zero (cost, time, ratings independent); investigate non-quantitative factors affecting satisfaction
  • Track restaurants exceeding 60-minute thresholds for intervention and support

6. Weekend Strategy:

  • Leverage strong weekend demand (69% of orders) with special promotions and limited-time offers
  • Partner with top weekend cuisines (American, Japanese) for exclusive weekend menus
  • Use weekend's faster delivery performance as a marketing point ("Get it in 22 minutes on weekends!")
